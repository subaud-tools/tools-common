## When to reach for it

- **A change you want built.** A feature answers *what should we build?* It goes from what the agent understood, through decisions, to a spec and plans you approve.
- **Something broken you can't explain.** An investigation answers *why does this happen?* The agent collects facts and tests one hypothesis at a time. Once the cause is confirmed, the fix goes to a feature plan.
- **An idea for a system of several parts.** A system design answers *what could this be?* You talk it through on the page while the agent tracks the topics, the settled lines and a diagram of the system so far. When a part is ready, it becomes a feature plan of its own.
- Any change you'd like to see before it's built, big or small: a button's behavior or a page layout as often as a redesign.
- A change with a real choice in it, where you want the options side by side with the agent's recommendation.
- Work that spans several repos or more than an afternoon, where the page keeps track of where things stand.

## When to skip it

- A one-line fix with one obvious answer: just ask your agent to make it.
- A question you want answered, not a change you want made.
- Reading around a subject with no change, fault or system in mind: just ask your agent.

## Where it sits

First. A change usually starts here: you plan on diazo's page, polish any writing in galley, the agent's workers build the plan, and the release reaches you through kempt. diazo also shows the build as it goes, so you can come back to the same page to see where things stand. When something is broken, an investigation comes first and hands its fix to a feature plan.

## Pointers

- **Pick the kind, or let the agent.** New plan's Kind starts at Agent picks. You can change it while the plan is in its start; after that, Start fresh as… in the plan's ⋯ menu opens a new plan with a link back.
- **Decide on the page, not in chat.** The agent posts each choice as a card with its options and its recommendation; you pick there. If it asks you a decision in the terminal instead, tell it: "Ask me that on diazo's page."
- **A feature grows in order.** First the agent says back what it understood, then the decisions, then a spec (what will change and why), then one or more plans of tasks. Each needs your approval before the next.
- **You can cut tasks.** Before you approve a plan, drop any task you don't want; it becomes a follow-up for later.
- **Your steps are listed.** Anything only you can do (a sign-in, a look at a page, a go-ahead to merge) shows under Your steps with how to do it.
- **Save an idea for later** by asking the agent to save it as planned; it waits on the front page without counting as work for you.
- **Share it.** The Record view shows the whole plan read-only, as one file you can send to someone.
