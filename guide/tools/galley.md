## When to reach for it

- Writing someone else will read: an answer to a customer, a guide, workshop text, a proposal, a blog post, a design doc, a README.
- A web page whose words need work: galley opens an HTML page's text for editing and redraws the page each round.
- A document you want to share looking finished: you edit plain markdown, and galley turns it into a well-formatted page that's easy to send.

## When to skip it

- Code: review it in a pull request instead.
- Text only your agent will read, such as notes for itself.
- A one-word fix you can make yourself in your editor.

## Where it sits

After the plan, before it ships. When a diazo plan produces writing (a README, a guide, a page), that's the moment to open it in galley. You can also start here with any draft, no plan needed.

## Pointers

- **Ask for a change** by selecting words: Add instruction appears beside them. With nothing selected, an instruction is about the whole document.
- **Fix small things yourself.** Type straight into the document; your edits stay, and the agent won't undo them.
- **Revise sends the round.** Your instructions and edits go to the agent together, and its rewrite streams into the page as you watch. Approve ends the review with the document as it stands.
- **Chat doesn't change the document.** The Chat tab is for questions; only instructions and Revise change the text.
- **Every round is kept.** History shows each version and what changed between them.
- **Press ? on the page** to see its keys.
- **If you press Revise and nothing arrives,** ask your agent: "Check galley's channel." It can see why the round didn't reach it.
