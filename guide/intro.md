# Getting started with subaud

## What subaud.tools are

subaud.tools is a small family of tools for working with an AI agent on your own machine. You talk to your agent in a terminal, as you already do; the tools give the two of you places to work together that a terminal can't: a page where the agent lays out the choices in a change and you decide (diazo), a page where you mark up a document and the agent rewrites it (galley), and a way to keep each piece of work in its own session with its pages beside it (cast). Each tool runs on your laptop and keeps its work on your disk.

## Why you're here

You don't learn these tools by memorising commands. You ask your agent for what you want, and it runs the tools; you decide on their pages. What takes practice is knowing which tool a piece of work calls for, and getting used to answering in either place: on the page or in the terminal, with the other side catching up. These lessons give you that practice on something small.

## What you'll make

A practice repo with a README, a notes file and a plan behind them: planned on diazo's page, built by your agent, and polished in galley. It lives in a folder of its own, `~/subaud/practice`, on your machine only, so nothing you try here can touch your real work, and you can delete it when you're done.

## How it works

Each lesson is a short list of steps on this page. Every step has one thing to ask your agent, with a button that sends it to the terminal beside this page. Your agent does the work there, and the step fills in with what it ran and what it means. When you've read it, press **Next**. From lesson 2 on, a tool's own page opens as a tab beside this one, and some answers you give there instead. A lesson ends with a recap.

## What you'll do, in six lessons

1. **What you have.** Your practice repo, the tools on your machine, and keeping them current.
2. **Your sessions with cast.** A project and its session, the split page, Chrome or Ghostty, and saving a layout.
3. **Plan with diazo.** An idea to an approved plan, deciding on the page or in the terminal.
4. **Build and finish with diazo.** Watch it built, do the step only you can do, sign off and share the record.
5. **Write with galley.** Revise the README in rounds, read what changed, approve and share it.
6. **diazo and galley together.** Tighten the plan's wording in galley and see diazo show the change.

Two more lessons wait for when they're useful: one on sessions talking to each other (muster), offered when you run two at once, and one on finding waste in a repo's CI (muda), offered when you work in a repo that has some.
